How I chose my translators (and why I picked people I know)

A picture book has so few words that every one of them matters. That changes what you look for.

By Jessica Sellars

I’m working on Spanish and Mandarin editions of my Echo books. Translating a picture book sounds simple because there are so few words. In reality, that is what makes it hard. Every word is doing a lot of work, and a translation has to carry the meaning, the warmth and the fun, not just the dictionary definitions.

What I looked for in a translator

  • A native speaker. Someone who grew up with the language knows how it sounds when it’s read out loud.
  • Experience with children. Words for kids have to be simple, clear and natural, without talking down to them.
  • Meaning over word-for-word. A good translator will tell me when a line doesn’t work in their language and suggest something that does.
  • Someone I can talk to easily. Translation is a conversation, with lots of questions in both directions.

The tricky bits

An ABC book is a special challenge. “A is for Adoption” only works if the word starts with the right letter in the new language, and Mandarin doesn’t use an alphabet at all. Rhyming books like Echo Goes to the Vet bring their own puzzle, because a rhyme in English almost never rhymes once it’s translated.

Why I went with people I know

Emma is translating the Spanish editions. She is my daughter’s friend’s abuela, and she has a long history of teaching and translating. She knows how kids learn, how they listen, and what makes them laugh, and she cares about getting it right.

Tong is translating the Mandarin editions. She is my sister-in-law and a native Mandarin speaker, and she can handle the Chinese characters herself, which is a big part of making the finished book look right.

Working with people I trust means I can ask every question, they understand what these books mean to me, and I know the care they put into each page is real. For a book about family, it feels right that family and friends are helping bring it to more readers.
