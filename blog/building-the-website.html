Building this website (and why it isn’t beige)

I write under two names for very different readers. I wanted one place that could hold all of it.

The story so far · Part 2 of 5

By Jessica Sellars · September 2026

I write picture books as Jessica Sellars and darker, stranger fiction for teens and adults as J.R. Deem. For a while I thought those needed two separate homes. The more I thought about it, the more it felt like splitting myself in half.

Picking a name

I needed one name that could cover an alphabet book about my dog and a slipstream novel about two people sharing one body. I landed on Waypoint Ink. A waypoint is a marker that tells you where you are on the way to somewhere else, and that’s exactly what each of these books is for me.

Before I fell too hard for it, I checked that the name was free everywhere I’d need it: the website address, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. It was. I took that as a sign. I also retired an older name I’d been using for the kids’ books, so now everything lives under one roof.

Building it

I am not a web developer. I built this site with the help of AI, one page at a time, and hosted it for free on GitHub Pages with a domain I bought myself. That meant learning words like “repository” and “commit” in the evenings after work. It was slow at first and then, suddenly, it wasn’t.

The part I cared about most

  • No beige. I really don’t like beige. The first draft of the kids’ page had a lot of it, so it became a soft blue.
  • Pinks, turquoise and peonies. Peonies are my favorite flower, so my About page got pink, turquoise and flowers.
  • Honest art labels. The images on my novel pages are concept art I use as a reference while I write. They’ll be replaced by a real illustrator’s work before any book is sold, and the site says so.
  • Something to give back. Free Echo coloring pages for kids, and a Start Writing page for anyone who wants to tell their own story.

The site keeps growing: book pages, coloring pages, my apps, and now this blog. Thank you for being here.
