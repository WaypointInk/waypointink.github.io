Making our own shirts with DTF transfers and a friend’s heat press

I ordered the transfers online. My friend brought the heat press. We made an afternoon of it.

By Jessica Sellars

I wanted shirts I could wear to readings and events, with my art on them. Instead of ordering finished shirts, I decided to try making them myself, with a lot of help from a friend.

The transfers

I ordered DTF transfers online from Jiffy. DTF stands for “direct to film.” Your design is printed onto a special film with an adhesive on the back. Then you use heat and pressure to press it onto the shirt, peel the film away, and the design stays on the fabric.

I liked that I could send in my own art and get back transfers that were ready to press. No screen printing and no special equipment, except one big thing.

The heat press

That big thing is a heat press, and my friend owns one. We spent an afternoon pressing shirts together, and it was so much fun.

What I learned

  • Follow the transfer instructions. Temperature, time and pressure all matter, and so does whether you peel the film hot or cold.
  • Press the blank shirt first. A quick press smooths out wrinkles and dries out moisture before the transfer goes on.
  • Measure your placement. A design that’s a little crooked is very noticeable once it’s on.
  • Do a test shirt. It’s better to learn on one shirt than on all of them.
  • Bring a friend. It goes faster, and it’s a lot more fun.
