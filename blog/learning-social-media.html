Learning social media, starting from ten followers

I didn’t want to buy followers or pay for promos. I wanted to meet people. That turned out to be slower and much better.

The story so far · Part 3 of 5

By Jessica Sellars · October 2026

My first post on @waypointink was about Echo’s ABC. In early October I had ten followers. Most of them knew me in real life.

No shortcuts

There are a lot of services that promise to “feature” your book or grow your account for a fee. I decided early on not to use any of them. I wanted the people who follow along to actually care about kids’ books, dogs and stories, so I set small goals instead: first 25 followers, then 100.

What I actually do

  • Show up a little every day. About half an hour of real engagement: following, reading and leaving honest comments.
  • Find my people. Parents of little kids, teachers and librarians, other children’s book authors and illustrators, dog lovers and rescue groups, and local businesses and bookstores.
  • Trade, don’t pay. Book swaps with other kidlit authors, where we each share the other’s books, have been one of my favorite things.
  • Plan ahead. I schedule posts in advance so a busy week at work doesn’t mean going silent.
  • Keep it real. I share human-made art and real moments, and I gravitate toward creators who do the same.

What surprised me

How kind everyone is. Read-aloud creators have offered to share Echo with their audiences. A local maker is trying bookmarks to go with my books. Authors I’ve never met in person cheer each other on every day.

I’m still learning. But I’ve stopped thinking of social media as marketing. It’s the place I meet the people who end up in my story times. If you’re a reader, teacher, author or fellow dog person, come say hi at @waypointink.
