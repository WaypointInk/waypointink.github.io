I’m joining the Reedsy Novel Sprint this November

Thirty days, fifty thousand new words, and absolutely no AI-written pages. I can’t wait.

By Jessica Sellars

Starting November 1, I’m taking part in the Reedsy Novel Sprint. The goal is simple to say and hard to do: write 50,000 new words of a novel between November 1 and November 30.

I have been looking forward to this for weeks. Part of it is the deadline. A finish line I can see is the best motivation I know. Part of it is knowing that a lot of other writers will be sitting down at the same time, chasing the same number, and cheering each other on.

How it works

  • 50,000 words in 30 days. That works out to about 1,667 words a day, every day.
  • Everything is written in Reedsy Studio. No copying and pasting from another program.
  • The words have to be new. You can bring in a draft you already started, but the 50,000 words have to be added during November.
  • No AI-generated writing. The book has to come from the writer.

That last rule made me smile, because it’s already how I work. Every word in my books is mine. (I wrote more about where I do and don’t use AI in my post about finding the right artist.)

Why a sprint suits my brain

I have ADHD, and long projects with no clear end are where I tend to drift. A sprint flips that. Thirty days is short enough to feel urgent and long enough to make real progress. A daily word count gives me something I can check off. I wrote about the other tools I’m building to stay on track in this post on writing with ADHD.

Follow along

I’ll be working on one of my J.R. Deem novels, and I’ll post word-count check-ins and the occasional small win on @waypointink. If you’re sprinting too, come say hi. We can keep each other honest.
