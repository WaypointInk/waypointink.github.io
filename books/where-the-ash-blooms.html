Prologue

What the Wind Carried

Every day they sent him out to watch for changes, and every day, as far as Cedar could tell, nothing changed at all.

He'd been doing this since he was young enough that the assignment had felt like an adventure instead of a routine, walk the reaches, note the land, watch for anything different from the day before. He was old enough now to know better. The grass out here was always the same green it had been yesterday. The black cedar stumps never moved, never would, standing exactly where they'd been standing for two hundred years and likely meant to stand for two hundred more. Nothing ever happened out here. He'd stopped expecting it to somewhere around his fourteenth year and simply kept coming anyway, because it was the job, and because some stubborn part of him still liked the quiet more than he liked admitting to.

He settled into his usual spot in the warm grass and pulled out his sketchbook, the way he did every single day, and began the slow, careful work of taking in every detail of the land spread out below him, the fold of the hills, the black glint of old stone, the particular lean of the grass where the wind favored it. Someday all of this would be a proper map, stitched together from a hundred afternoons just like this one. Today was, by every indication, going to be exactly like all the others.

Then the wind shifted, and brought something with it that had no business being here.

Sweet. Warm. Something like flowers pressed into wax, carrying farther than any scent had a right to on open ground like this. Cedar lifted his head, sketchbook forgotten in his lap, and turned his face into the wind trying to place it. He knew every plant that grew wild in this stretch of the reaches, knew their smells the way he knew his own hands, and this wasn't any of them. This was something made.

He packed up without fully deciding to. Curiosity had never been part of the job description, not officially, but the habit of chasing down anything unaccounted-for had been drilled into him too deep to argue with now.

He followed the scent quiet and low, the way he moved through anything he didn't yet understand, and it led him along a fold in the land he'd walked past a hundred times without ever once suspecting what it hid. A rise, unremarkable from every angle he'd ever approached it from before. And there, tucked into the side of it, half-swallowed by grass and old stone, a door.

Cedar went still and watched from cover, heart going faster than the moment strictly warranted.

A girl came into view a moment later, moving fast, purposeful, a pack slung over one shoulder, and disappeared through that same hidden door before he'd gotten more than a few seconds' look at her. Dark hair, he registered, distantly. Quick on her feet. Gone before he could catch anything more than the impression of her.

The door closed behind her, and the hillside went back to looking exactly as unremarkable as it had a minute ago, and Cedar stood there a long while just staring at the place where a door had been.
